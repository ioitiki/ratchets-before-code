## Inferred (not proven by anything above)

- **Durability against power loss.** `kill -9` is a process crash: the OS still holds the page cache and writes it out. No test cuts power or crashes the OS. `synchronous = FULL` is set by `Store::open`, and nothing here reads it back.
- **One kill point is tested: mid-effect.** The other windows are covered only by the core's recovery examples, with no real kill:
  - after the lease and before the intent: `a_lease_whose_effect_never_started_is_retried_whatever_its_kind`
  - after the outcome and before the `Succeeded` event: `a_finished_effect_completes_without_running_again`
  - between journaling recovery's events and marking the old intents `abandoned` inside `recover_from_restart`: not tested at all. From reading the code, the next start finds the job no longer `Leased`, leaves it alone, and records the `abandoned` outcome then.
- **The non-idempotent branch through the server.** "Killed, not repeated" is proven for `Notify` only by the core example `an_unfinished_non_idempotent_effect_is_killed_not_repeated`. No test kills a real server mid-`Notify`: its effect has no duration to kill into.
- **The crash test separates startup recovery from lease expiry by timing alone.** With recovery turned off, the job would still be re-driven once its 30-second lease expired. That path also gives attempt 2 and a single `Succeeded`. The mutant failed only because the test's 30-second wait ran out first. From the constants, not from a measurement: re-driving after expiry finishes about 33 s after the lease (30 s lease, 1 s backoff, 2 s effect). The wait ends about 30 s after the restart. So the mutant would pass if the gap from the lease to the restarted server's first poll exceeded about 3 s. On this machine the whole test took 3.2 s.
- **What "exactly once" means here.** The test proves exactly one `Succeeded` event in the journal. The idempotent effect itself ran twice: attempt 1 was killed partway through its sleep, and attempt 2 ran in full. That is the intended semantics for an `Idempotent` kind (ADR 0003), not a single execution.
- **Time to success.** The 3.05 to 3.07 s from kill to success is consistent with the 1 s backoff after attempt 1 (`backoff_ms(1)`), then the 2 s effect, plus polling. The phases were not timed separately.
- **What no test checks:**
  - The `abandoned` outcome that recovery records for the killed attempt is not asserted.
  - The 500 response for a store error is not exercised.
  - The `http` test helper assumes hyper closes the connection on `Connection: close` and sends a non-chunked body. Every run above worked; nothing else checks it.
- **A single node.** `recover` assumes any lease alive at startup belongs to a dead process. Nothing stops two `ledgerd` processes from opening the same journal. Inferred from the code; not tested.
- **This branch's CI run.** The report is committed before the push. Until now CI's `crash-recovery` job has skipped its steps. It has not yet been observed running the test on `ubuntu-latest`, where the timing margin above may differ.
